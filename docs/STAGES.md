## LLM calls: the AI gateway

Every model call (the posters and collection judges, tonight's picks) goes through the Vercel AI Gateway's
OpenAI-compatible Chat Completions API (`https://ai-gateway.vercel.sh/v1`) on one key, `secrets/ai_gateway_api_key`,
read on every request. `[llm]` in the config sets the gateway and each task; `config.example.toml` lists the defaults.

| Task | Default model, then fallback | Reasoning | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| `posters` (movie, show, season judge) | `alibaba/qwen3-vl-instruct`, then `alibaba/qwen3.7-flash` | none | vision; the bake-off's best rank correlation, and its fallback at a tenth of the price |
| `collections` (franchise and director judges) | the same | none | vision; director photos were not in any bake-off |
| `tonight` (daily picks) | `deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash`, then `alibaba/qwen3.7-flash` | off | text; the judges' first choice, and the most reliable and cheapest |

The vision tasks send effort `"none"`, not `"off"`: `"off"` asks for the least the *first* model allows, and
qwen3-vl-instruct has no reasoning control, so nothing would be sent and qwen3.7-flash, answering as the fallback,
would reason. Both models answered a live call with `"reasoning": {"effort": "none"}` (2026-10-06T02Z, HTTP 200; 0
reasoning tokens). tonight's `"off"` sends effort `"none"` too, which both of its models list.

Each task may list `fallbacks`: the gateway tries them in order when the model fails (its `models` field), and the
answer names the model that served it. A task table that leaves `fallbacks` out keeps the default's; `fallbacks = []`
means none. A Claude model is just another id (`anthropic/claude-sonnet-5.5`); there is no
second client.

**Throughput and failure policy** (`internal/llm`):

- at most `in_flight` (8) calls in the air across the process; posters fans out that wide;
- each attempt has `timeout` (120 s); 429, 5xx, timeouts and dropped connections are retried with jittered backoff
  (2-4 s, 4-8 s, 8-16 s) or after the gateway's `Retry-After`, up to `attempts` (4) in all; a `Retry-After` over 5
  minutes fails the call at once; any other answer (400, 401, 404) fails it at once;
- a stage's run parks a task after `park_after` (5) failed calls in a row: no more calls this run, one alert;
- a billing refusal (402; or a 403 for no card on file, `customer_verification_required`, or for a model outside the
  free tier, "Free tier users do not have access to this model") parks the task at once. It is kept in the store
  (since when, the last refused call, whether it was told) until a call is answered again, and while it stands:
  - one alert in all, naming since when, raised by the first stage that meets it (`TellBilling`, the stored "told"
    pattern of the scheduler's once-only alerts); later runs, other stages and restarts only log
    `judge_billing_held` / `tonight_billing_held`;
  - no call is sent but one an hour (`billingRecheck`), across the process and its restarts: every other session is
    parked before it calls (posters writes no ledger row for it), and the callers that reach the hourly try wait for
    its answer, going on if it was answered and held if it was refused again (callers already waiting then get a $0
    `failed` ledger row). So a refused account costs at most 24 free calls a day, and the stages resume within an
    hour of the owner fixing billing;
  - it is no model failure: it bills nothing, holds nothing against a budget, and does not count towards a title's
    unusable answers;
- an answer that cannot be read is asked once more in the same run; posters then marks the title unjudged (two
  unusable answers, as before); tonight keeps its one-retry rule. A "reason" inside "scores" is ignored, not fatal.

**Cost and budgets.** A call's cost is the gateway's own figure when the answer carries one, else its usage at the
dearest price the gateway may bill for the model. That price is the highest of the model list's prices (`GET
/v1/models`: base, regional, fast and tiered) and every provider's (`GET /v1/models/<id>/endpoints`), each at its peak
multiplier; both are fetched once a day for every model a task names and cached in the store, since the gateway picks
the provider. The providers matter: a live call to `deepseek/deepseek-v4-flash-0731` (2026-10-06T01:12Z) was served by
alibaba and billed $0.44 / $1.32 per million tokens, its $0.22 / $0.66 doubled in its peak window (00:00-14:00 UTC),
where the model list says $0.13 / $0.26. Before a call, its worst case (the input estimate, at 3 bytes a token plus each
image's 28-pixel patches, and all of `max_tokens` as output, at the dearest price among the task's models) is reserved
against the stage's cap: posters' `budget_usd` and `collection_budget_usd`, tonight's `daily_cap_usd`. An attempt sent
in full whose answer was lost counts at that worst case even when a retry answers. `posters_calls` records the model
that answered, its tokens and its cost.

The reservation is one attempt's worst case, as specified, so a cap is hard for new calls but not for lost answers: a
call that loses answers after sending them in full counts each lost attempt at its worst case, so the spend can pass the
cap by at most `in_flight` × (`attempts` − 1) worst cases, and the next call is then refused. For posters on
`alibaba/qwen3-vl-instruct` (dearest $0.40 / $1.60 per million, alibaba's; its fallback's dearest tier is $0.20 /
$0.80) that is 8 × 3 × $0.0021 ≈ $0.05: a six-candidate title estimates about 1,270 input tokens (387 for the prompt,
6 × 126 image patches for 252×392 thumbnails, the framing) plus all 1,024 output tokens. On the dearest bake-off model, `alibaba/qwen3.8-27b` ($0.99 /
$4.35), it is about $0.14. tonight's pair is sized the same way and logs `tonight_over_cap` when lost answers pass it.

The cost figure is `provider_metadata.gateway.cost` (the message's, else the answer's). The Chat Completions docs
(fetched 2026-10-06) show no cost field, but the live answer of 2026-10-06T01:12Z carries it as a decimal string
(`"cost": "0.00000572"`, beside `marketCost`, `inferenceCost` and the routing), and `usage.cost` as a number. It is read
when it is a number or a decimal string, with or without "$"; any other shape (an object, text with units, a metadata
that is not an object) counts as no figure, never as an unreadable answer that would be asked again and paid twice.

**The bake-off** (`cmd/bakeoff`, a tool, not a test) sends each candidate model exactly what the stage would: 60 titles
drawn from a copy of the production store with stored Sonnet verdicts (owner picks and Plex defaults; uploads are never
judged, so none has one), movies, shows and seasons, plus hard cases (alt art he picked that Sonnet ranked lower, a
pool with a non-English poster, verdicts that turned on text or clutter); and tonight's prompt as the stage sizes it.
It reports per model: % valid answers, top-1 agreement with Sonnet (a tie with Sonnet's pick agrees), the mean Spearman
of the scores, how often its top pick is one Sonnet scored 4 or less, cost per title, p50/p95 latency, and a contact
sheet of its disagreements; for tonight, validity and the picks with their reasons. Its doc comment has the commands;
`-fake` runs it on the fake gateway, and `-max-usd` (default $1) caps a live run. A title not sent (past `-max-usd`, or
held by a billing refusal) is counted in a "not sent" column, never as an invalid answer or a $0 title: validity and
cost per title count the titles sent. The owner's picks are drawn with rule `owner_pick` too (fix/batch-1 stores them
so, Sonnet's scores kept). The client keeps its catalogue and any billing refusal in `<out>/llm.db`, not in the
production copy, so a refusal stored there cannot hold a run back after billing is fixed.
